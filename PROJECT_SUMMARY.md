# Project Summary: Quantum-Enhanced Evolutionary Computation — A Bilateral Research Initiative between India and Austria

## Overview

This bilateral research project establishes a collaborative scientific partnership between the Heuristic and Evolutionary Algorithms Laboratory (HEAL) at the Upper Austria University of Applied Sciences, Campus Hagenberg, led by Prof. Dr. Michael Affenzeller, and the Department of Mathematics and Computing at Dr. B R Ambedkar National Institute of Technology (NIT) Jalandhar, India, led by Dr. Anupam Yadav. Conducted under the India-Austria Bilateral Scientific and Technological Cooperation Programme, the initiative explores the convergence of two transformative computational paradigms: quantum computing and evolutionary algorithms. Over a 24-month period, the project aims to generate new algorithms, theoretical insights, software tools, and a lasting international research partnership at the frontier of quantum-enhanced evolutionary computation.

## Background and Motivation

Quantum computing has moved rapidly from theoretical abstraction to engineering reality. Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) devices now available from IBM, Google, IonQ, and others offer tens to hundreds of qubits, enabling practical experiments in quantum-enhanced optimization, quantum machine learning, and hybrid quantum-classical algorithms. The fundamental quantum mechanical phenomena that underpin these devices—superposition, entanglement, and quantum interference—allow quantum processors to explore exponentially large state spaces in ways that have no classical counterpart. The leading near-term approach—variational quantum algorithms such as the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) and the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA)—combines parameterized quantum circuits executed on hardware with classical outer-loop optimizers. These hybrid architectures create natural and largely unexplored integration points for evolutionary computation, since gradient-based parameter optimization suffers from the barren plateau problem on deep circuits, while population-based metaheuristics are gradient-free by design.

At the same time, evolutionary algorithms have matured into a powerful family of metaheuristic optimization methods capable of navigating complex, high-dimensional, and multimodal search landscapes where classical mathematical programming fails. Modern evolutionary computation encompasses genetic algorithms, differential evolution, genetic programming, evolutionary strategies, and swarm intelligence, all supported by rich theory and decades of industrial practice. These methods are particularly valuable when the fitness function is non-differentiable, stochastic, or expensive to evaluate—all characteristics shared by many quantum computing optimization tasks.

The intersection of these two fields—quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms (QIEAs) and evolutionary methods for quantum computing—is a rapidly growing but still immature research area. Despite more than 200 QIEA variants documented in the literature since Han and Kim's seminal work in 2002, critical gaps remain: insufficient rigorous benchmarking against modern classical baselines, limited open-source engineering platforms, underdeveloped theoretical foundations, and sparse cross-community knowledge transfer between quantum physicists and evolutionary computation researchers. This project directly addresses these gaps by combining HEAL's world-class expertise in algorithm engineering and empirical methodology with NIT Jalandhar's expertise in novel metaheuristic design and mathematical analysis.

## Research Objectives

The project pursues five interconnected scientific objectives. The first is to design novel quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms that integrate quantum-bit encoding, quantum rotation gate operators, superposition-based diversity mechanisms, and entanglement-inspired crossover structures into classical evolutionary frameworks, with demonstrable performance improvements on benchmark and real-world optimization problems. The second is to develop evolutionary approaches for the automated design and parameter optimization of quantum circuits, targeting variational quantum algorithms and quantum machine learning models, with the goal of evolving circuit architectures, rotation parameters, and gate sequences that minimize circuit depth and maximize performance. The third objective is to explore hybrid quantum-classical evolutionary frameworks in which quantum processors act as co-processors within evolutionary optimization loops—providing quantum-enhanced fitness evaluation, quantum-assisted population initialization, and quantum-accelerated genetic operators—and to assess their computational advantage on suitable problem classes.

The fourth objective is to conduct rigorous theoretical analysis of the convergence behaviour, computational complexity, and scalability of quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms, developing mathematical frameworks that clarify when and why quantum inspiration genuinely improves over purely classical methods. The fifth objective is to extend the HeuristicLab open-source optimization platform with quantum-inspired algorithm modules and quantum simulator interfaces, and to carry out comprehensive empirical benchmarking that establishes rigorous experimental methodology for this emerging field.

## Methodology and Work Plan

The project is structured across three phases. During the first phase (Months 1–6), both groups conduct a joint literature survey, hold bilateral knowledge-transfer seminars, and collaboratively define a focused set of 3–5 specific research problems, benchmark suites, and evaluation criteria. This phase culminates in the submission of a state-of-the-art review paper.

The second and core phase (Months 7–18) is devoted to algorithm development, implementation, and theoretical analysis. On the quantum-inspired side, the teams design novel algorithms incorporating quantum-bit representations, quantum gate rotation mutations, and quantum entanglement-inspired crossover operators, implementing them within HeuristicLab's modular architecture. On the evolutionary quantum computing side, genetic programming and grammatical evolution are applied to evolve quantum circuit structures, while metaheuristic methods inspired by Dr. Yadav's Artificial Electric Field Algorithm are adapted for variational parameter tuning in VQE and QAOA settings. A bridging software framework connects HeuristicLab with leading quantum simulators—Qiskit, Cirq, and PennyLane—and with cloud quantum hardware via IBM Quantum and Amazon Braket. Theoretical investigations develop convergence proofs and complexity analyses for selected algorithm classes, and begin to distinguish genuine quantum advantages from classical simulation of quantum concepts.

The final phase (Months 19–24) focuses on comprehensive benchmarking using standard optimization test suites (CEC benchmarks, BBOB functions, combinatorial instances) and real-world application cases from both groups' domains, followed by publication, open-source software release, and strategic planning for the project's continuation.

## Researcher Mobility and Exchange

Researcher exchange is the project's central mechanism for knowledge transfer and collaborative innovation. Four planned mobility visits span the project's duration. In the initiation phase, Dr. Yadav visits HEAL Hagenberg for three weeks to achieve deep familiarity with HeuristicLab's architecture and HEAL's algorithm engineering practices and to collaboratively identify research problems. In the active development phase, a HEAL postdoctoral researcher spends three weeks at NIT Jalandhar for collaborative algorithm design and quantum computing knowledge transfer. Later, an NIT Jalandhar doctoral student visits HEAL for six weeks of intensive implementation work and training in empirical evaluation methodology. In the consolidation phase, Prof. Affenzeller visits NIT Jalandhar for two weeks to co-lead a bilateral workshop and engage in strategic planning. These visits are complemented by bi-weekly virtual meetings, shared GitHub repositories, and quarterly milestone reviews throughout the project.

## Complementarity of Partners

The scientific complementarity between the two institutions is a central strength of this initiative. HEAL contributes over two decades of experience in evolutionary algorithm engineering, a mature software platform used internationally for metaheuristic research, deep expertise in genetic programming and symbolic regression, established methodologies for statistical comparison of stochastic optimizers, and extensive links to Austrian industry in production, logistics, and bioinformatics. NIT Jalandhar contributes creative algorithmic innovation rooted in physics-inspired and biology-inspired principles—exemplified by the Artificial Electric Field Algorithm—strong mathematical foundations for theoretical analysis, expertise in swarm intelligence and soft computing, growing capabilities in quantum computing, and access to India's rapidly expanding quantum technology ecosystem.

This combination is precisely what advancing quantum-evolutionary computation requires: engineering rigour to build reliable, scalable, and reproducible platforms, paired with mathematical depth and inventive algorithm conception. Neither group could as readily provide both dimensions independently; together, they form a highly synergistic collaboration.

## Expected Scientific Outcomes

On the algorithmic front, the project expects to deliver 3–5 new quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithm variants with clear empirical performance profiles across benchmark problem classes, evolutionary quantum circuit optimization methods validated on variational quantum algorithm tasks, and a hybrid quantum-classical evolutionary framework interfacing with real and simulated quantum hardware. On the theoretical front, convergence and complexity results will provide formal underpinnings for the most promising algorithmic approaches.

The software platform deliverable—open-source HeuristicLab extensions released under GPL v3—will give the international research community production-quality implementations for experimentation and reproducibility. A standardised benchmark suite and open data policy will support fair comparison across future studies. The survey paper is expected to serve as a foundational reference for the field.

## Dissemination and Impact

The dissemination plan targets 4–6 journal papers in high-impact venues including IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation, Evolutionary Computation, Swarm and Evolutionary Computation, and Quantum Science and Technology, along with 4–6 conference presentations at GECCO, CEC, PPSN, and IEEE Quantum Week. All publications will be made available as open-access preprints on arXiv. Two bilateral workshops—one in Hagenberg and one in Jalandhar—will be open to the broader research community, and the teams plan joint tutorials at major conferences to introduce the field to new audiences.

Industry relevance is maintained through presentations at HEAL's technology symposia, engagement with India's quantum technology sector through NIT Jalandhar's networks, and development of quantum-enhanced optimization methods for production scheduling and logistics. At least two joint follow-up grant applications to EU Horizon Europe, FWF Austria, DST India, and SERB India are planned.

## Capacity Building and Long-Term Partnership

The project trains at least four early-career researchers—two doctoral students and two postdoctoral fellows—in interdisciplinary quantum-evolutionary methods through direct research collaboration and international exchange. The doctoral students will benefit from joint supervision arrangements, exposing them to research cultures, methodological traditions, and technical infrastructure at both institutions. Postdoctoral researchers will gain experience in collaborative international science, contributing to the growing pool of researchers capable of working across the quantum computing and evolutionary computation communities. Jointly developed educational materials, tutorials, and training modules will be suitable for integration into graduate curricula at both institutions and will be publicly released to maximise their reach. A formal collaboration agreement between the Upper Austria University of Applied Sciences and NIT Jalandhar will provide an institutional framework for continued student exchanges and research cooperation well beyond the project period, ensuring that the personal relationships formed during the project are supported by lasting structural links between the two institutions.

## Strategic Alignment

The initiative directly addresses strategic priorities at both national and institutional levels. India's National Quantum Mission and Austria's Quantum Austria initiative both identify quantum technologies and artificial intelligence as central to future scientific and industrial competitiveness. By building human capital and scientific infrastructure at the intersection of quantum computing and evolutionary computation—two rapidly advancing fields each designated as strategic priorities—this project creates durable value for both nations. The structured mobility programme, open-science commitments, and clear intellectual property arrangements ensure that the benefits of the collaboration are maximally shared, fairly attributed, and effectively communicated to scientific, industrial, and policy audiences in both countries. The project also contributes to the broader goal of strengthening science and technology ties between India and Austria, serving as a model for how bilateral cooperation schemes can catalyse frontier research that neither country would readily pursue in isolation.

In summary, this bilateral initiative represents a timely, strategically motivated, and scientifically well-founded investment in a frontier research area. The complementary expertise of HEAL and NIT Jalandhar, the structured 24-month work programme, and the shared commitment to algorithmic innovation, engineering rigour, and open science together provide a strong foundation for meaningful scientific advances and a lasting Indo-Austrian research partnership. The project is designed not merely as a short-term exchange but as the seed of a sustained collaborative programme: the publications, software platform, trained researchers, and follow-up grant applications generated during the 24-month period are all intended to create momentum that will carry the collaboration forward into larger-scale projects. By addressing fundamental questions about the relationship between quantum computation and evolutionary search while simultaneously building the practical infrastructure—software, benchmarks, trained personnel, and institutional agreements—needed to pursue those questions at scale, the project will continue to produce impactful results long after the formal project period concludes and will stand as a productive model for future India-Austria scientific cooperation.
