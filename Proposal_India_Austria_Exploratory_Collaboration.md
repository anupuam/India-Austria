# India–Austria Bilateral Research Initiative

## Title
**Exploratory Collaboration on Quantum-Inspired Evolutionary Algorithms: Building the Foundation for a Joint Research Programme**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This proposal outlines an exploratory bilateral collaboration between research groups in India and Austria in the area of quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms (EAs) and their applications to complex optimization problems. Funded through a bilateral travel grant, this initiative will serve as an **incubator**: bringing together researchers from both sides through joint online and in-person meetings, identifying promising research directions and concrete master thesis topics, and preparing the ground for a future competitive joint research grant application.

The scientific focus — the intersection of Evolutionary Algorithms and Quantum Computing — is timely, rapidly evolving, and well-suited to joint India–Austria expertise. Rather than committing to deliverables that go beyond the realistic scope of a travel grant, we propose to use this funding period strategically: to build mutual understanding, align research agendas, and develop a shared research vision that can be translated into a strong full grant proposal.

---

## 2. Background and Motivation

Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs) — including Genetic Algorithms, Differential Evolution, and Evolution Strategies — are among the most powerful general-purpose metaheuristics for hard optimization problems. A growing body of work explores how quantum computational principles (superposition, entanglement, quantum-inspired amplitude updates) can enhance or inspire new EA variants. This intersection is scientifically rich, with open questions spanning theory, algorithm design, and applications.

Both the Indian and Austrian research groups bring complementary expertise:

- **Indian side**: strong background in evolutionary computation, multi-objective optimization, and simulation-based optimization.
- **Austrian side**: expertise in computational intelligence, metaheuristics, and growing engagement with quantum information research.

A sustained joint research programme in this area is a natural next step, but requires a period of careful alignment — on research questions, methodology, and publication strategy — before committing to a full multi-year project.

---

## 3. Objectives of the Exploratory Phase

The primary objective of this travel-grant-funded phase is **relationship building and agenda setting**. Concretely, we aim to:

1. **Establish a shared understanding** of the state of the art in quantum-inspired EAs and identify where the most impactful open problems lie.
2. **Run joint online seminars** in which each group presents its current work, allowing both sides to identify complementary strengths and potential synergies.
3. **Hold at least one in-person meeting** (alternating between India and Austria) to deepen collaboration and conduct focused working sessions.
4. **Identify and agree on two to three master thesis topics** that can be pursued in parallel at both institutions, creating a first layer of joint scientific output.
5. **Draft a roadmap** for a future joint grant application (e.g., to DST–FWF bilateral calls or EU-India research programmes), including a shared list of target research questions and potential application domains.

---

## 4. Scientific Scope

The scientific discussions during this exploratory phase will focus on the following themes, selected for their feasibility and mutual interest:

- **Quantum-inspired representation and operators**: How quantum concepts such as amplitude encoding and rotation-based update rules can be translated into practical, hardware-independent EA enhancements.
- **Multi-objective formulations**: Identifying problems where quantum-inspired EAs offer genuine advantages in multi-objective settings.
- **Surrogate-assisted optimization**: Combining quantum-inspired search with computationally cheap surrogate models for expensive fitness landscapes.
- **Benchmark design**: Jointly developing benchmark problems that meaningfully test the added value of quantum-inspired EA components.

These themes are chosen to be tractable within a master-thesis timeframe and to naturally grow into PhD-level and grant-funded research directions.

## 6. Master Thesis Topics (Proposed)

The following are illustrative thesis directions to be finalised collaboratively during Q1–Q2:

1. **Quantum rotation-inspired mutation strategies in Differential Evolution**: Empirical study on benchmark suites with theoretical analysis of diversity preservation.
2. **Multi-objective optimization using quantum-amplitude-based selection pressure**: A comparative study against classical Pareto-based approaches on standard benchmarks.
3. **Surrogate-assisted quantum-inspired EAs for expensive optimization**: Design and evaluation of a surrogate model pipeline compatible with quantum-inspired population update rules.

Each thesis will be co-supervised by one advisor from each institution, creating genuine academic exchange and a foundation for longer-term collaboration.

---

## 7. Pathway to a Full Research Grant

This exploratory phase is explicitly designed as a stepping stone. A successful incubator period is expected to yield:

- A **jointly authored position paper or survey** presenting the shared research agenda (target: workshop paper or invited contribution at a relevant venue).
- A **full joint grant proposal** to DST (India) – FWF (Austria) bilateral call or equivalent, building directly on the aligned research agenda and preliminary results from the master theses.
- A **network of researchers** — including master students who may continue as PhD candidates — that forms the human infrastructure of the longer-term programme.

Experience from similar bilateral initiatives suggests that a focused exploratory phase significantly increases the competitiveness of subsequent full grant applications by demonstrating established collaboration, existing joint outputs, and a realistic and well-scoped research plan.
